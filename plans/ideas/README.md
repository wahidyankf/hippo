# Ideas

An idea is two pages of argument, not a delivery plan. It exists so a proposal can be judged before anyone builds it: what the problem is, what evidence there is that it is real, roughly what could be done, and what would have to become true before the idea is worth promoting.

One kebab-case Markdown file per idea, in the quadrant its evidence supports. Read this index before adding one; two briefs proposing the same thing are worse than one, because each is now half the argument.

## Shape

In order: an H1 title, a one-line summary, and a dated provenance note; the problem and its evidence; why now; prior art, with direct links and the date each was read; the proposed direction as a sketch; rough scope and non-goals; risks and open questions; and what success would look like, with the signal that would promote the brief.

Leave out file-level design, Gherkin, and delivery checklists. A brief carrying those is a plan that skipped being reviewed as one. When the important unknowns are answered, replace the brief with a formal plan folder in [`../backlogs/`](../backlogs/README.md) that carries its evidence and its decisions forward.

## Quadrants

Urgency and importance are read off dated evidence rather than off how the work feels this week. An idea with evidence for neither belongs in Q4 until it has some.

## Directory Map

- [Q1 — urgent and important](q1-urgent-important/README.md) holds work with an immediate stake and real consequence.
- [Q2 — important, not urgent](q2-not-urgent-important/README.md) holds valuable work under no deadline.
- [Q3 — urgent, not important](q3-urgent-not-important/README.md) holds time-boxed work of limited consequence.
- [Q4 — neither urgent nor important](q4-not-urgent-not-important/README.md) holds deliberately parked ideas.
